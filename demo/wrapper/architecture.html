The problem

Spatial analysis ends in a dead document.

How it works today

  • Enterprise needs site or portfolio risk analysis
  • Hires a GIS consultant or specialist team
  • Weeks later: a static PDF and some screenshots
  • The deal team argues about it over email
  • The lender gets a document nobody can interrogate

The cost

  • Slow, expensive, one-shot
  • No collaboration on the actual output
  • No way to ask "what about this property instead?"
Every team that makes decisions with spatial analysis has this problem. The analysis happens in specialist tools; the discussion happens everywhere except on the map.
What you pay a consultant for

The consulting process, step by step

  1. Read the academic literature: which criteria matter, which weights are defensible
  2. Source authoritative data: federal datasets, not vendor black boxes
  3. Build the model: criteria, weights, exclusions
  4. Validate against ground truth
  5. Deliver a report with methodology documentation
This process is rigorous. It is also entirely repeatable. Nothing in steps 1 through 5 requires a human to do it twice.

What the platform needs

Gap analysis from building on our own APIs

  • No comment-creation API: collaboration can't be seeded programmatically
  • No viewport-controlled static map render: PDF snapshots are limited to auto-framed thumbnails
  • Layer updates require a full re-upload or Enterprise Live Data: monthly portfolio re-scores are awkward, and comment threads stay behind on the old map
  • Maps accumulate one per analysis run: archival and cleanup need tooling once usage is real
These aren't complaints. They're the roadmap for making Felt the default canvas for operational spatial workflows.

The wrapper took days, not quarters, because the platform did the hard part. That's the pitch to every workflow builder out there, and the reason wrappers should be a first-class part of our commercial story.
